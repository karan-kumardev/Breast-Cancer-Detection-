Is this Cancer suspicious?

Four mammograms the model has never seen are scored by a ResNet18 model. Each score is compared with a cut-off that was chosen to find at least 80% of cancers.

The score is the model's output, not a true probability. This is a classroom project and not a medical device.

How it works

  1. PrepareThe image is turned grey and resized to 224 × 224 pixels, the same as the training images.
  2. ResNet18A network pretrained on everyday photos, then fine-tuned on mammograms, gives one score.
  3. CompareThe score is compared with a cut-off of {{ (cutoff * 100)|round|int }}%, chosen on validation data so at least 80% of cancers are found.
  4. DecideAbove the cut-off the image is flagged for review. Below it, it is not flagged.

Every model uses a cut-off chosen on validation data to reach about 80% recall. The 95% ranges of the ROC-AUC values overlap, so the data does not show one model is clearly better than the others.

Limitations

  • Modest accuracy. At this cut-off about 1 in 5 cancers is missed and more than half of the benign images are flagged.
  • Small images. Mammograms were shrunk to 224 × 224 pixels, so fine details are lost.
  • Possible shortcuts. The model may react to brightness, tissue density or film labels instead of the tumour itself.
  • One dataset. It was trained on Mini-DDSM only and has not been tested on images from other scanners or hospitals.
  • Not medical advice. This is a classroom project. Never use it to make a health decision.
